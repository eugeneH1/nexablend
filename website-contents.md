# Nexablend — website copy brief (Project North)

This document is **reference material for a copywriting or layout agent** who will refresh marketing website text. It contains **product facts, positioning, audiences, and example hooks** drawn from internal product documentation. It is **not** final copy: you choose tone, headlines, section order, and phrasing for the site.

**Important:** Rand amounts, transaction limits, and some module combinations in internal docs are **illustrative or in flux**. Use **plan names, tier logic, and outcome language** on the public site. Sync any **exact prices, caps, or legal claims** with the live billing and legal teams before publishing.

**Out of scope for public website copy:** implementation details, repository layout, programming frameworks, database design, or internal codenames for engineering (for example, do not expose internal routing keys to customers). The customer-facing brand is **Nexablend**. **Project North** is the program or company umbrella behind the product; use it on the site only if brand strategy calls for it—default customer language to **Nexablend**.

## Elevator pitch (fact layer)

Nexablend is a **business operating system** for small and growing businesses in the **real economy**—especially where **power and internet are not always reliable**. It helps owners and teams **sell**, **get paid**, **see what is happening** (stock, appointments, customers), and **grow** without juggling disconnected tools. The product is designed to stay **usable when connectivity drops or the grid cuts**, then **sync cleanly** when conditions improve.

---

## Problems Nexablend is built to address

Use these as **problem → outcome** scaffolding for headlines and body copy.

1. **Hostile everyday infrastructure** — Load-shedding, patchy connectivity, and costly data mean many tools “work on paper” but fail at the till or in the field. **Outcome:** fewer lost sales and less rework when the environment is unstable.

2. **Fragmented tools** — Point of sale in one place, customers in chat apps, stock in a notebook, invoices in templates, staff in group messages. **Outcome:** one place for customers, catalog, sales, payments, and history.

3. **Owner as entire back office** — The same person sells, orders stock, chases payments, and tries to stay compliant. **Outcome:** structured workflows and visibility so the owner can spend more time on revenue and less on admin archaeology.

4. **Compliance and payroll pressure** — Tax, payroll, and audit expectations grow with the business; enterprise-grade help is often unaffordable or absent. **Outcome:** paths from simple starts toward exports, payroll, and stronger reporting as the business matures (exact scope depends on tier and activated modules).

5. **Software that assumes rich back offices** — Many products assume stable power, always-on internet, and dedicated admin staff. Nexablend assumes the opposite for its core audience. **Outcome:** “it works here” credibility with township, peri-urban, and lean teams—not only suburban offices.

## What every customer gets (universal core)

Regardless of tier, the platform is built around a **shared foundation** (internally called “Core”). In customer language, that means:

- **Customers** — Profiles, history, tags where relevant, and a single view of who buys what or books what.
- **Catalog** — **Products and services** in one model. Services can carry duration (for example a 45-minute treatment) even before full scheduling is turned on.
- **Sales and payments** — Create orders, take payment (cash, card, integrated payments where offered), issue receipts, retain transaction history.
- **Messaging and documents** — Templates and notifications for receipts and customer communication (email, SMS, WhatsApp-oriented workflows where productized).
- **Dashboard** — Same-day visibility: sales, top lines, simple trends so the owner answers “how was today?” without spreadsheets.
- **Team and safety** — Roles for staff (who can sell, who can refund, who sees reports), plus audit-style traceability for sensitive actions.

**Walk-in first:** A service business can run on this foundation with **walk-ins** and manual coordination (phone, WhatsApp) until they are ready for full **staff calendars, online booking links, and automated reminders**—those are expansion moments, not day-one barriers.

## Modules and “growing into” power

Advanced capabilities are described internally as **modules** or **add-ons** so businesses only pay for complexity when they need it.

- **Bookings and scheduling** — Staff calendars, availability, reminders, public booking, deposits where offered. Natural when appointment volume or no-shows hurt revenue.
- **Point of sale and inventory** — Till-style selling, stock ledger, low-stock signals, returns. Natural when stock-outs, shrinkage, or manual counts hurt margin.
- **Finance and tax (where offered)** — Invoices, expenses, reporting, exports toward accountants or statutory tools.
- **Payroll** — Employee records, payslips, statutory reporting orientation (market-specific). Natural when the first hires appear.
- **Multi-location** — Branches, transfers between sites, consolidated view. Natural when a second site opens or stock moves between depots.

Tier packaging decides **which of these are included, bundled, or sold separately**; the **story** for the website is: start simple, add capability when the pain is real—without changing to a different “universe” of products.

## Nexablend — Tier 1 (starter / general SMB)

### Who it is for

- **Retail-only** — Corner shops, spazas, boutiques, small food takeaway counters.
- **Service-only** — Mobile mechanics, tutors, tradespeople, solo practitioners who mostly sell time.
- **Hybrid** — Salons, barbers, spas that sell **treatments and retail products**; any business where “we sell both” is normal, not an edge case.

Planning docs stress: **hybrid is essential**, not optional—many service businesses also move product.

### What makes Tier 1 different in one sentence

**One adaptive product** whose emphasis shifts based on what the business sells (products, services, or both)—not three disconnected products.

### Plans (names and roles — not prices)

Internal naming for base subscriptions:

- **Free** — Entry for trying the system and running a very small operation; usage limits apply (exact limits are product/legal, not this doc).
- **Pro** — Paid step that mainly **lifts limits** and supports heavier daily use (more transactions, larger catalogs, more room for customers and users—exact numbers from live product).
- **Business Pro** — **Bundle positioning** for businesses that already stack several paid capabilities or need **higher limits and stronger reporting** in one package; journey narrative: “you are paying for multiple pieces—here is one bundle that matches how you actually run.”

### Add-ons (customer benefits)

| Add-on (internal name) | Customer-facing idea |
|------------------------|----------------------|
| Bookings & scheduling | Calendars, reminders, online booking link, staff availability. |
| Inventory (with POS) | Stock tracking, till flow, low-stock awareness, returns discipline. |
| Payroll | Hire staff, payslips, year-end employee documents where market rules apply. |
| Multi-location | More than one site: branches, stock movement between locations, rolled-up reporting. |

### Adoption story (for “how it fits my life” copy)

1. Owner hears about Nexablend through **community channels** (for example WhatsApp, referrals, partner offers).
2. Starts **free or low-friction**, runs daily sales and a simple customer list.
3. Hits a **real trigger**: runs out of stock, needs appointments, hits a usage cap, opens another branch, hires—then **adds a module** or **upgrades plan** while staying in the **same product family**.

## Nexablend Pro — Tier 2 (professional / vertical)

### Who it is for

Established or regulated businesses that need **industry-shaped language and workflows**, not only generic retail and service:

- Insurance brokers and advisors  
- Real estate agencies and property managers  
- Medical and dental-style practices (multi-practitioner clinics, physios, and similar)  
- Auto workshops and panel shops  
- Vehicle dealerships  
- High-touch salon, spa, and barber businesses that live on scheduling, retail shelf, and client preferences  

### Positioning line

**“Purpose-built for your industry”** — Marketing may use **industry suites** (for example Insurance Brokerage Suite, Medical Practice Suite) as **package names**. Under the hood, planning describes **one professional product** with industry-specific emphasis; customers should experience **coherent vertical software**, not “generic ERP with a skin.”

### Contrast with Tier 1 (for comparison tables)

- **CRM** — Deeper customer and pipeline workflows than “starter” CRM.
- **Invoicing** — Strong professional billing story alongside retail receipts.
- **Inventory and scheduling** — Described in roadmap as **typically included in the professional bundle** (whereas Tier 1 often treats them as add-ons). Confirm live packaging before promising specifics on the site.

### Industry packages — feature idea bank for copy

The bullets below are **planning-level capabilities** to inspire section copy, FAQs, and industry landing pages. **They are not a shipped checklist**; product marketing must validate what is live before absolute claims (especially integrations with third parties, regulators, or medical schemes).

**Insurance Brokerage Suite — brokers, advisors**

- Client portfolios with multiple policies per client  
- Renewal pipeline and reminders (for example 60 / 30 / 7 days)  
- Commission tracking and multi-insurer reconciliation  
- Document vault for policies, IDs, proof of address  
- Compliance-oriented tracking (for example FAIS, CPD themes)  
- Customer alerts on channels the business already uses  
- Insurer connectivity where productized  

**Real Estate Agency Suite — agencies, property managers**

- Listings with status and media

**Auto Workshop Suite — mechanics, panel shops**

- Vehicle records linked to customers  
- Job cards (labor + parts)  
- Parts inventory and supplier ordering  
- Service reminders to customers

**Auto Dealership Suite — dealers**

- Vehicle stock with photos and specs  
- Trade-in valuation support  
- Finance quote experiences for buyers  
- Lead and test-drive pipeline

**Salon & Spa Suite — salons, spas, barbers, studios**

- Staff scheduling and service menu with durations and tiers  
- Online booking link  
- Retail POS for products used or sold in-salon  
- Client preferences and notes  
- Before/after or portfolio imagery where appropriate

## Nexablend Enterprise — Tier 3

### Who it is for

- **Wholesalers, distributors, importers** — High volume, credit customers, routes, B2B ordering.  
- **Manufacturers and assembly** — Recipes, batches, raw materials, yield and quality themes.  
- **Multi-branch retail or franchise-style networks** — Control plane across many sites, not only a second shop.  
- **Anyone whose workflow diverges** from “single storefront + catalog” enough to need a **dedicated enterprise surface** (planning allows more than one enterprise “shape” over time).

### Themes for enterprise story (directional)

Use as **vision and roadmap language** until product confirms GA:

- **Multi-branch command** — Aggregated performance, comparisons across regions or stores, role of a regional or head-office view.  
- **Stock movement between sites** — Transfer requests, visibility of surplus and shortage across the network.  
- **Manufacturing and true cost** — Bill of materials: selling a finished item drives consumption of raw materials; landed cost including freight and duties for importers.  
- **Compliance at scale** — Payroll batches, statutory file generation, employee document delivery, fewer manual bank runs.  
- **B2B and supply chain** — Portals for retailers to order from wholesalers, tiered pricing, credit terms, route planning; long-term ideas like supplier visibility into downstream stock (vendor-managed style narratives) appear in vision docs—treat as **directional** unless shipped.

### Go-to-market

- **Sales-led** conversations, demos, onboarding projects.  
- **Hook angle:** replace a **fragmented stack** of spreadsheets, legacy systems, and disconnected branch tools with **one resilient operating layer**.  
- **Price band (illustrative only):** internal grids mention **roughly R2,999+ per month** style entry for enterprise positioning; **do not publish numbers** without approval.

## Companion app (mobile)

**Intent for marketing**

- Nexablend is planned as an ecosystem where the **same business** can be run from **the browser** and from a **companion mobile experience** for people on the move (field staff, drivers, owner on the shop floor without a PC).
- The **canonical services** behind the product are designed to accept **web and companion** traffic with **shared sign-in** so users are not maintaining two unrelated identities.

**What to say carefully**

- The **primary mass-market experience** is a **fast, installable web application** that is intentionally resilient for uneven connectivity.  
- A **dedicated native-style companion** is part of the **roadmap and platform strategy** for deeper engagement and Tier 2 / Tier 3 credibility (staff in the field, quick captures, notifications).  
- **Do not** promise a specific app store listing, feature parity matrix, or offline behavior on mobile **unless** product management has published that factsheet.

**User-facing essence (safe line):** “Run the business from the counter or from your pocket—one account, one source of truth.” Adjust when the mobile app launches.

## Geography and local fit (customer-facing)

- **Primary mental geography:** South Africa and similar **Global South** markets where **infrastructure, data cost, and informal-to-formal business transitions** shape daily operations.  
- **Channels:** Heavy use of **WhatsApp** and mobile-first habits; receipts and confirmations should feel native to how customers already communicate.  
- **Payments:** Support for **local payment habits** (cards, instant payment rails, QR-style flows where integrated)—exact brands and methods depend on integrations; say “South Africa–aware payments” unless marketing lists approved partners.  
- **Compliance:** Tax, payroll, and reporting are **ongoing anxieties**; position Nexablend as **reducing surprise and manual reconstruction** rather than replacing accountants or legal advisors.

## Why customers adopt Nexablend (differentiators for copy)

1. **Continuity** — Keeps working when the environment is imperfect; syncs when back online.  
2. **Simplicity first** — No need to face “enterprise complexity” on day one.  
3. **Growth path without migration trauma** — Add modules, upgrade plans, later move into **industry** or **enterprise** positioning while staying in one conceptual product family.  
4. **One source of truth** — Customers, catalog, sales, and stock live together; fewer disputes and less double entry.  
5. **Built for how small businesses actually grow** — Free or low entry where strategy allows, upsell tied to **real triggers** (stock pain, hiring, second location, renewal chaos), not abstract feature shopping.
